# **Episode 7:**

## *Overexertion*

The rain dwindled.

A steady, monotonous drip.

Sliding down thick vines.

Choking the shattered window frame.

Inside, the air remained heavy.

Thick with the damp smell of wet stone.

And the lingering, metallic tang of raw meat.

His stomach had stopped its hollow aching.

The psychological toll remained.

Eating like an animal left an invisible residue.

A dull friction scraping against a mind.

A mind that could not remember who it used to be.

He pulled the worn notebook from his pack.

The pages parted.

Revealing his previous observations.

The strange, defiant preservation of the ruins.

The impossible precision of the stonework.

Beneath them, his pencil moved with deliberate friction.

Adding new lines to the ledger of his survival:

-   Sustenance secured.
-   A lean, brown-furred quadruped.
-   Uncooked, unpalatable, but effective.
-   The structures contain unidentified artifacts.
-   Some functional; some hazardous.
-   Verdict on shelter:
-   The ruins are a trap that offers sanctuary.
-   The walls that keep the outside world at bay.
-   Could just as easily become a tomb if flooded or cornered.

He stared at the final sentence.

The graphite heavy against the paper.

The paradox offered no comfort.

Closing the cover with a soft snap, he tucked the book away.

Shouldered his spear.

And pushed back out into the gray aftermath of the storm.

The detour back to the cave was necessary.

The gear left behind.

The blunt club.

The rusted iron sword.

And the heavier tools he had lacked the capacity to carry.

They could not be left in the open.

Working quickly.

Half-sliding down muddy embankments.

He gathered the remaining weapons.

And hauled them back toward the safety of the ruined city.

Each trip felt like a gamble against time.

As he hauled the last bundle of heavy steel across the broken threshold, the last bruised light of dusk began to bleed out of the sky.

The shadows between the buildings stretched.

Deepening into absolute black.

A sudden, chilling prickle crawled up the back of his neck.

The lake.

The memory returned with sudden, violent clarity.

Silent, four-legged shapes moving through the dark on his first night.

The low, calculated cadence of their footsteps.

If I don't secure these doors before absolute dark...

I am entirely finished.

Adrenaline surged.

Clearing the fatigue from his bones.

He moved with frantic, calculated speed.

Barricading the broken frame with fallen timber and heavy stones.

Until the gaps narrowed to mere splinters of light.

Inside the suffocating quiet of the makeshift fortress, he sank back against a cold stone pillar.

Chest heaving.

Lungs burning for clean air.

Silence settled over the room.

Heavy.

Absolute.

Then

A flicker of movement caught the edge of his vision.

His head snapped toward the barricaded opening.

Framed in the gloom.

Peering through a narrow gap in the broken wood 

Two points of luminescence burned with unnatural intensity

Unusually bright

Golden

Watching him.

The realization hit him like a physical blow.

They hadn't lost his scent.

They had simply been waiting for him to stop moving.

His muscles locked.

A final wave of exhaustion and overwhelming shock crashed over his nervous system.

The spear slipped from his numb fingers.

Clattering against the stone floor.

As darkness rushed inward to swallow him whole.

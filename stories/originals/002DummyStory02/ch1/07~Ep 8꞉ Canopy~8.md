# **Episode 8:**  

## *Canopy*


The midday heat cut through the chill of the ruins.

He woke to blinding strips of sunlight slicing through the gaps in the timber barricade.

His heart hammered a frantic rhythm against his ribs for several seconds.

Before his brain finally caught up to the empty room.

He sat up.

Sweat plastered his hair to his forehead.

The floor around him was undisturbed.

No claw marks.

No blood.

Not a single physical trace to prove that two glowing golden eyes had been staring at him through the dark just hours ago.

It was almost worse than an attack.

The silence felt deliberate.

A reminder that whatever moved through these streets could come and go entirely at its own leisure, completely bypassing his crude defenses.

He pushed himself up to his feet, wincing as stiff muscles protested the movement.

The stone walls of the ruined house suddenly felt less like a sanctuary.

And more like a stone box waiting to be locked from the outside.

He looked up.

Through the shattered remnants of the upper floor and the tangled canopy of old-growth trees punching through the decaying architecture, the high branches offered a completely different kind of perspective.

Ground level was a hunting ground.

The creatures owned the dark, the alleys, and the earth.

But there must be some limits to them too.

The thought crystallized in his mind.

If the ground was compromised, the solution wasn't to build a stronger box on the dirt.

The solution was to leave the dirt behind entirely.

He spent the next few hours working with quiet, methodical focus.

The gear he had hauled back from the cave the previous evening—the heavy tools and the rusted iron blade—finally proved their worth.

He selected a massive, centuries-old hardwood tree that had grown directly through the center of a collapsed stone courtyard, its thick trunk anchored securely within the ancient masonry while its upper branches stretched far above the surrounding rooftops.

Climbing it was exhausting work.

Every muscle strained against the vertical pull, his palms raw as he hauled himself upward from branch to thick, overlapping branch, testing each foothold before trusting his weight to the wood.

By late afternoon, high above the damp stone and the lurking shadows of the forgotten city, he had carved out a crude but effective platform wedged securely among the upper boughs.

It wasn't much.

Just a series of heavy branches lashed together with sturdy vines and reinforced with wedged timber.

Yet, sitting there with his legs dangling over the open air, looking down at the sprawling, silent maze of ruins below him, the constant, prickling dread in the back of his neck finally began to ease.

The wind up here was cleaner.

The view stretched out over the broken skyline, revealing distant green ridges and the gray ribbon of the lake far in the distance.

Up here, the world felt vast instead of enclosing.

For the first time since he had woken up in this strange, forgotten place, he felt a quiet sense of control settling into his hands.

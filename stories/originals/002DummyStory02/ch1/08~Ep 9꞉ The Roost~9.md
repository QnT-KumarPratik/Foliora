# **Episode 9:**

## *The Roost*

The descent was necessary.

Stomach hollow, throat dry, he had to climb back down to scour the ruins for whatever rations he had left.

The midday heat offered a brief, deceptive window of quiet.

No glowing eyes.

No heavy footsteps tracking his movements in the dirt.

Just the oppressive, baking silence of the dead city.

He secured what little food he could find, his movements rushed and anxious, his eyes constantly darting toward the gaping shadows of alleyways and broken doorways.

By the time he hauled himself back up the rough bark of the hardwood, the air was already beginning to cool.

The sun was sinking, sliding down through the sky toward that critical, invisible threshold—the exact angle where the blinding gold softened into a heavy, bruised amber-red.

That was when the canopy came alive.

At first, it was just a sound.

​A leathery, dry rustling that rippled through the dense foliage above like a sudden gust of wind through dry paper.

​Then they appeared.

​Massive, heavy-bodied shapes dropping from the upper branches.

​Skinny, leathery-skinned things with long, elongated finger-bones stretching wide into webbed wings—distorted arms that were somehow wings, yet still unmistakably arms. They had wet, gleaming eyes, stretching and folding themselves as they woke from the day's sleep.

​His breath hitched.

​No. Not here.

​This wasn't a treehouse anymore.

​It was an active roost, and he had just built his living room right in the middle of their front porch.

​Panic flared.

​He gripped his heavy wooden club, swinging it frantically in short, defensive arcs to clear the air around him, desperately trying to avoid actually striking the writhing shapes.

​He didn't want to provoke a panicked swarm.

​He just wanted space.

​Thud. Flutter. A sharp, high-pitched shriek.

The club felt impossibly heavy in his sweating palm.

In his haste to dodge a swooping shadow, his boot caught the uneven edge of a lashed branch.

His foot slipped out over empty air.

His stomach violently lurched into his throat.

For a terrifying split second, his entire body tipped outward over the sheer drop, looking straight down at the unyielding stone courtyard far below.

A desperate, lunging grab saved him, his fingers clawing into the thick bark of the central trunk as he hauled himself back onto the platform, gasping for air, his heart slamming against his ribs like a trapped bird.

Going down now was out of the question.

His arms were trembling from the frantic club-swinging, his muscles spent, and the darkness below was already bleeding into the streets.

Trying to climb down a vertical trunk in the pitch black while half-exhausted was a guaranteed way to break his neck.

He had no choice.

Cursing under his breath, shaking with a mix of adrenaline and profound disgust, he abandoned the club.

He grabbed his spare cloak and heavy cloth wrappings, pulling them over his head, wrapping himself tightly from boot to crown, and curled into a miserable, suffocating ball in the corner of the platform.

He stayed completely still.

Listening to the heavy, leathery thud of wings brushing past his ears all night long.

Listening to the scratching of sticky, hooked feet pacing mere inches from his face.

The morning light cut through the gloom the next day.

He woke up stiff, freezing, and choking on a thick, acrid musk.

He peeled the heavy cloak away, blinking against the pale dawn.

The creatures were gone, dissolved back into the daytime shadows.

In their place, the entire platform, his gear, and the thick folds of his cloak were heavily coated in a fresh, glistening layer of foul-smelling guano.

His confidence in the canopy didn't just fade.

It leaked away in the damp morning air.

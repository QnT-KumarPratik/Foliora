# **Epsopde 1:**

## *The Awakening*

Darkness pressed against his eyelids.

Voices echoed somewhere beyond his reach, their words splintering into meaningless fragments before they could be understood. Blood stained his vision in scattered crimson spots while a deep, resonant hum rattled through his chest like distant thunder. He struggled to move, to remember, to simply open his eyes.

They refused.

When they finally parted, blurred faces hovered above him. Some cried openly. Others stared in silence. None of them were familiar.

His throat burned with dryness. He swallowed.

The world changed.

Another blink.

The tears running down their faces had become blood.

Another blink.

The people themselves twisted apart, flesh vanishing without a sound until only skeletons remained.

Then...

Silence.

The nightmare dissolved.

He opened his eyes again.

This time there was no blood. No impossible visions. Only a damp cave illuminated by faint light creeping through its entrance. Rough stone surrounded him on every side, cold enough to bite into his skin. Every muscle protested as he forced himself upright.

His head pounded.

His memories were worse.

Whenever he reached for them, they slipped away like water through open fingers. Faces. Voices. Pain. They existed only as fading fragments without names or meaning.

He didn't even know who he was.

The cave offered no answers.

Leaning against the stone wall, he slowly made his way toward the pale light spilling through the entrance. Each step felt heavier than the last until he finally emerged into daylight.

A small lake rested nearby.

He knelt beside it and looked into its still surface.

A stranger looked back.

The face was somehow familiar, yet completely unknown, as though he had spent a lifetime wearing it without ever truly seeing it.

There was nothing else to learn from the water.

He returned to the cave.

This time he searched carefully.

Skeletons lay scattered across the floor, their remains reduced to brittle bone by countless years. Whatever tragedy had happened here had happened long ago.

Among the remains, one pouch had survived.

Inside were dried supplies, several strange glass vials filled with unfamiliar liquids, and assorted tools whose purpose escaped him completely. None of it stirred even the faintest memory, but abandoning useful equipment seemed foolish.

He gathered everything into a lone backpack lying deeper within the cave.

Before leaving, he quietly arranged the scattered skeletons together. It wasn't much of a burial, but it felt wrong to leave them as they were.

Continuing farther into the darkness, the cave suddenly betrayed him.

The ground disappeared beneath his feet.

He crashed hard against rough stone before managing to climb back out, bruised but alive.

Eventually he reached the entrance again.

Taking one final look around the cave, he stepped outside.

The world waiting for him felt...

Wrong.

The air carried a strange weight.

The trees looked almost familiar, yet subtly different, as though they had been recreated from a fading memory by someone who'd never seen a real forest before.

Even the ground felt foreign.

Looking back into the cave, the truth became impossible to ignore.

Everything left behind had decayed into dust.

Whatever had happened to him...

Time hadn't simply passed.

It had raced ahead without him.

Near the cave entrance, he emptied the contents of the backpack.

A worn sword.

A spear.

Several odd metallic tools unlike anything he could identify.

Glass vials filled with cloudy liquids that shimmered strangely beneath the sunlight.

He examined each item carefully, hoping something would awaken his lost memories.

Nothing did.

Choosing a weapon proved no easier.

He swung the sword.

Awkward.

He practiced with the spear.

Equally terrible.

Not a single movement felt natural.

If he had ever been a warrior, his body had forgotten along with his mind.

Still, he continued practicing.

One swing became ten.

Ten became a hundred.

Hours vanished unnoticed until the sky slowly darkened.

Only then did reality remind him of a far more immediate problem.

Night was coming.

He needed shelter.

His choices were few.

The cave, where he had lain unconscious for an unknown amount of time.

The lakeside, peaceful but completely exposed.

Or the trees, difficult to climb but perhaps safer once above the ground.

None of the options inspired confidence.

But the world no longer seemed interested in offering good choices.

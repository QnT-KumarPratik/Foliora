# **Episode 10:**

## *Five Centimetres*

The morning sunlight did little to improve the situation.

He sat straight up in the high branches and immediately regretted breathing.

The smell was staggering.

A thick, heavy reek of damp musk and foul waste clinging directly to his skin.

He needed it gone.

Climbing down from the wood, he kept low, eyes scanning the broken stones for anything to wipe himself with.

That was when he caught a sharp, clean scent cutting through the rot.

Green shoots pushing up through cracked pavement. Small, clustered leaves. Different from the rot, smelling cool and sharp against the heavy air.

He reached down and grabbed a stem.

Ouch.

Tiny, needle-sharp points flared across his palm like hot iron, followed by a vicious, burning itch.

He yanked his hand back, staring at the sudden red marks blossoming across his skin.

> "...Why."

He backed away, eyeing a different plant nearby to soothe the sting.

Thick stem.

Oozing a thick, pale fluid.

He smeared a bit of the liquid across his wrist, hoping to coat the skin.

The skin instantly blazed. Bright crimson. Swelling with violent, searing heat.

The pain tore through his fogged mind so fast, so sharp, that his jaw flew open.

A ragged, gravelly sound ripped out of his throat.

A voice.

His own voice. A harsh, broken noise echoing off the stone.

He froze, staring at his own hand, completely startled by the sound he had just made. He wasn't silent. He could make noise.

Panic spiked over the pain. He dropped everything and bolted.

The wide, grey expanse of water at the edge of the ruins.

He broke through the treeline and threw himself straight into the shallows.

The shock of the cold water hit his skin like a physical blow.

He gasped, sinking waist-deep, scrubbing frantically at his arms, his face, his hair. The crust dissolved. The burning on his skin faded into a dull ache.

He stood there in the chilling water for a long time, staring at his clean hands.

Water equals less terrible.

He was astonished and amazed.

Until his stomach growled, loudly demanding food.

He dragged himself out, found his hidden wooden spike, and set out into the treeline.

Patience. Waiting behind a stone pillar. A clean thrust. The meek brown creature dropped.

His second prey, a successful hunt.

He hauled the heavy carcass back toward the trees.

Walking through a patch of dry, waist-high brush on his way home, a strange scratching sensation caught his legs.

He looked down.

Dozens of small, hooked seed pods were plastered across his cloth wrappings like a second skin.

He pulled one off. Another hooked into his finger. Within seconds, every inch of his fabric was armored in aggressive, clinging burrs.

He stood there in the quiet woods, staring.

They weren't trying to hurt him. They were just using whatever passed by to spread across the dirt.

Back at the base of his tree, he looked at the heavy carcass. Then at his seed-covered wrappings.

He tried to pick a cluster off his sleeve. It dug deeper into the fibers, jabbing his knuckle.

Frustrated, he grabbed the hem of his tunic.

He pulled it up.

He slipped his arms out of the sleeves.

He dropped the tangled garment onto the dirt.

He stood there in the open air.

Free of the pricking weight.

He blinked.

Clothing wasn't a permanent second skin bolted to his body.

It came off.

He stared at his own discarded shirt for a long, quiet moment.

He walked over to his first shelter, and quietly added a few clumsy scratches to his hidden notebook.

Day 5

Water: good.
Plants: painful.
Noise: possible.
Animals: food.
Everything else: uncertain.

He had just advanced another five centimeters.
